# Multicenter Validation of an AI MRI Habitat Model for Identifying Unfavorable Regression Before Brachytherapy in Bulky Cervical Cancer

Xiaoxian Yea,1, Xuhao Daia,1, Ning Luoc,1,Xiaofang Lid, Pengrong Loua, Jiangping Rena, Jianliang Zhoua, Jiming Yanga, Yingying Zhoua, Yang Zhoue,***, Peng Xieb,**, Qian Wub,*,2



Bulky locally advanced cervical cancers may regress incompletely or eccentrically after external beam radiotherapy, increasing subsequent brachytherapy complexity. We developed a pretreatment MRI habitat-radiomics model using 464 patients from five institutions, including independent external validation in 101 patients. Voxel-wise local features from T2-weighted MRI were clustered into habitat subregions and compared with clinical, whole-tumor, peritumoral, and combined models. Unfavorable regression comprised stable or progressive disease, or eccentric partial response requiring interstitial brachytherapy, defined on contrast-enhanced MRI after 20–25 fractions by a >2-cm perpendicular offset from the cervico-uterine axis. The habitat model achieved AUCs of 0.894, 0.891, and 0.855 in training, internal validation, and external validation, respectively, exceeding the external AUCs of the whole-tumor (0.570), peritumoral (0.626), and clinical (0.675) models. The combined model added little discrimination (AUC, 0.858). Pretreatment habitat analysis may therefore capture spatial heterogeneity relevant to residual tumor geometry and brachytherapy planning.

**Keywords:** artificial intelligence; brachytherapy; cervical cancer; habitat radiomics; machine learning; magnetic resonance imaging
